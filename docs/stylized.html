Stylized

Pick the colours first, then take detail out until the drawing is the thing that reads. Everything on Building a world still applies; this is what changes.

The five moves

  1. Palette before texture. Five to eight colours, and every layer graded into them.
  2. One roughness band. Clamp it. Sparkle is noise, and noise is the enemy of a read.
  3. Cavity carries the shading. Bake it, then push its contrast further than you would for realism.
  4. Normals do form, not detail. Broad shapes, detail weighted right down.
  5. Vary hue, not value. Value variation muddies a palette; hue variation enriches it.

Palette

Grade layers to the palette rather than repainting the textures. Every layer has the same set of controls, so one grass texture can serve three biomes.

<Layer>_HueShift, Saturation, Value, Contrastpull the source art onto the palette
<Layer>_Tintthe palette colour itself
MacroTintA, MacroTintBtwo palette colours the macro noise crossfades between across the world
GlobalHue, GlobalSaturation, GlobalValue, GlobalTint (landscape)the whole terrain at once - a season, a time of day, a region
UE's Color Ramp node cannot be edited without recompiling the base material. Gradient Tool gives you gradients you can drag in realtime, which is how the Valorant and TF2 techniques are actually authored.

Roughness: one band

A stylized surface is not describing a real material, so a wide roughness range buys nothing and costs clarity. Clamp every layer into a narrow band and let cavity do the variation.

RoughnessMin / RoughnessMax0.35 / 0.6
Specular0.35 - lower than the physical 0.5, so highlights stay off the silhouette
DistanceRoughnessFloor0.5
DistanceNormalFlatten0.75

The last two matter more here than anywhere: with FXAA and no temporal filter, sub-pixel specular crawls, and a stylized scene has nothing busy enough to hide it.

Cavity carries the shading

Cavity is baked, the same way AO is and off the same high poly, but it reads tight creases and edge curvature rather than broad occlusion. It multiplies base colour and specular. A flat palette has given up most of its shading detail, so this map is doing more work here than it would in a realistic scene - bake it deliberately and then push it harder.

CavityContrast1.6
CavityColorAmount0.8
CavitySpecularAmount0.7
Cavity is also the height source the blend resolves against, so the same creases decide where the next layer comes through. A cavity bake with clear, chunky forms gives clear transitions for free.

Normals: form, not noise

High-frequency normal detail fights a flat palette. Keep the broad forms and weight the fine layer down.

NormalIntensity0.7
DetailScale / DetailStrength6 / 0.25

Interlock hard

A stylized transition should look chosen. Push the interlock until the boundary reads as a shape rather than a fade.

BlendHeightAmount0.85
BlendContrast0.75
BlendHeightAmount / BlendHeightContrast (landscape)0.7 / 0.7

Vary hue, not value

Value variation across a palette turns it to mud. Hue variation is what makes a stylized world feel painted rather than tiled.

Variation_HueRange14 degrees
Variation_SaturationRange0.18
Variation_ValueRange0.06
Variation_MacroTintAmount0.7
Variation_MacroValueAmount0.1

On the landscape, do the same to depth: DistanceDesaturation 0.25, DistanceColor set to a sky colour from the palette, DistanceColorAmount 0.35. That is aerial perspective, painted rather than fogged.

Foliage

Light through a leaf is the single most stylized-looking thing the foliage master does. Push it further than physics would.

SubsurfaceColora saturated palette green, brighter than the leaf itself
TransmissionAmount1.0-1.5
WindStrength / WindSpeed9 / 0.9 - large and slow reads as animation, not as jitter
WindFlutterStrength / WindFlutterSpeed3.5 / 4

Weather as a colour shift

Realistic wetness darkens and tightens the specular. Stylized wetness should mostly change the colour, because a palette can survive a hue move and cannot survive going dark.

Wetness_Darkening0.35
Wetness_RoughnessTarget0.2
Wetness_SpecularTarget0.55
Accumulation_NoiseAmount0.25 - cleaner snow shapes, less speckle
Accumulation_Facing0.45 - snow climbs further round a form

Checklist

  • The palette exists as an asset, and every layer's Tint came out of it.
  • No layer has a roughness range wider than 0.3.
  • Cavity was baked from the high poly, not generated from the normal map as an afterthought.
  • Hue range is larger than value range on every variation.
  • Nothing sparkles at 30 m. Check it at ES3.1 with FXAA on, moving.
  • The scene reads in the Layer Weights debug view as clear shapes, not a gradient.
