Foliage

A separate master, because two-sidedness, the shading model and the blend mode are material properties rather than parameters. Point a second recipe at the same output path with a different asset name and bFoliage on.

The pack

ParameterContentCompression
Layer0_BCalbedoDefault, sRGB
Layer0_NRMtangent normalNormalmap
Layer0_CRTR cavity, G roughness, B transmissionMasks, sRGB off
OpacityMaskread from alpha only - point it at Layer0_BCDefault, sRGB

Blue is the only channel whose meaning depends on the master. A surface master reads it as metallic; a plant is never metal, and that sample is paid for either way, so a foliage master reads it as thickness. There is no thickness map and no subsurface map: thickness is that channel, and the colour of the light coming through is SubsurfaceColor, one parameter for the whole plant, shaped per texel by the mask.

OpacityMask is read from alpha, not a colour channel. A greyscale mask in RGB samples as 1 in alpha and clips nothing, or as 0 if the texture has no alpha at all, and clips everything.
The mask is its own tap, not a swizzle of the base colour one, so a foliage master samples four textures where a one-layer surface master samples three. Pointing it at the same texture as Layer0_BC is the normal thing to do and does not make it free.

Transmission

Light through a leaf, per texel, from the channel a plant has no use for as metallic. This is the single biggest thing separating foliage that reads as a plant from foliage that reads as cardboard.

bTransmissionMapuse the CRT blue channel. Off by default
SubsurfaceColorthe colour of the light coming through. Default 0.15, 0.35, 0.08
TransmissionAmountglobal scale, default 1
LayerN_TransmissionScaleper layer, which is what makes bark and leaf on one mesh work
If foliage goes flat and dark the moment bTransmissionMap is ticked, the pack in Layer0_CRT has metallic in blue rather than transmission, and metallic on a plant is 0 - so no light gets through. Repack it: Mat → Remap Texture Channels…, target CRT.

Layers

Most foliage uses layer 0 alone. Layers 1 and 2 are for bark against leaf on one mesh, which is where per-layer TransmissionScale earns its place: the leaf lets light through, the stem does not.

Wind

Two scales of motion on world position offset, weighted by height above the object pivot so the base stays planted and the tips move. Vertex red scales the whole thing, so a mesh can say which parts are allowed to bend.

Rustle

Foliage pushed aside by what moves through it. A disturber is a sphere in world space, not a footprint on the ground, because a canopy is four metres up and a top-down field cannot tell walking under a tree from walking through it. Every plant inside the sphere leans away and rings; a plant a metre outside does not know it exists.

Setting one up

  1. Turn on Rustle on the foliage recipe and set its Rustle Collection. Generate.
  2. Point Project Settings → Plugins → Mob Rustle → Parameter Collection at that same collection.
  3. Add a Mob Rustle Disturbance component to anything that should part the grass.
On the componentDefault
Radius170 cmwider than the body. A sphere sized to a capsule only grazes the lowest leaves, which reads as nothing happening
RestStrength0.2how hard something barely moving pushes
MoveStrength1what a sprint through a bush looks like
ReferenceSpeed500 cm/sthe speed at which the push has reached MoveStrength
bEnabledonturns it off without unregistering

For a one-off - an arrow, a landing, an explosion - call AddRustleImpulse on UMobRustleSubsystem with a location, a radius and a push vector. No component needed.

There are four slots. The disturbers nearest the camera win. That is the right answer for a duel and the wrong one for a crowd, so do not put a disturber on every AI in a battle.
Project settingDefault
ImpulseLifetime2 show long a one-shot holds its slot. A slot released early is a plant that snaps straight rather than settling
ReleaseLifetime2 show long a slot goes on being written after its disturber has gone. What leaves a bush has to leave it moving
SettleSpeed20 cm/sbelow this a disturber counts as standing still, so grass held aside by somebody standing in it closes over them rather than staying parted

Cost

Texture taps4 for one layer - three plus the mask
Samplers4, as everywhere. Every tap goes through the shared wrap group
Overdrawreal. With r.EarlyZPass=0 there is no depth prepass, so masked geometry pays for every layer it draws through. This is the cost that actually matters for foliage
Wind and rustlevertex work, not pixel work. Both write the same WPO pin
The cheapest thing you can do for foliage performance is not in this material. Fewer, larger cards with tighter silhouettes beat more, smaller ones every time on a renderer with no depth prepass.

Checklist

  • The foliage master is a separate recipe, not a switch on the surface one.
  • OpacityMask points at a texture that actually has alpha.
  • Layer0_CRT blue is transmission, not metallic, and the shape reads against the sun.
  • Wind direction matches whatever the sky and the rest of the scene say it is.
  • Rustle disturbers exist on the player and a handful of others, not on everything.
  • Cards were reduced before the shader was blamed.
