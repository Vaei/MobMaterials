Techniques

Each of the expensive-sounding features, what it is actually for, when it is the wrong answer, and which number to turn to get a specific result. Surface and Landscape list every parameter; this page decides which ones you should be touching.

Triplanar

Samples the texture three times, once per world axis, and weights the three by the surface normal. World-aligned, so it needs no UVs at all and survives non-uniform scale.

Use itDo not
Kitbashed and boolean geometry with no usable UVsAnything with good UVs. It is 3x the taps for no gain
Rocks and cliffs, where a seam would be obvious and UV work is expensiveAnything rotated and non-uniformly scaled - it is world-aligned by definition and does not turn with the object, so the texture swims
Modular kits reused at many scales, so one material serves all of themMeshes that need the texture to follow a form, like a face or a fabric fold
Terrain-matching props, so the world grain runs continuously through themFoliage cards
Tune
TriplanarScaleworld units per UV. 1 ÷ tile size in cm. Set it from your texel density, not by eye
TriplanarSharpnesshow narrowly the three projections crossfade. 4 is a good default. Lower blurs a wide band across every 45° face; much higher makes the transition a visible line
Normals are reoriented per projection rather than blended flat, so a triplanar surface keeps its normal detail on all three axes instead of going soft where they meet.

Per layer, not per material. A rock can be triplanar on its base layer and mesh-UV on the moss layer painted over it.

Killing repetition

Four tiers, per layer, chosen by static bools so an unused tier is not in the shader at all. They go up in cost and in how completely they work. Pick the cheapest one that solves what you can actually see.

TierTaps
None1straight sample
Cheap1modulated by the macro noise the master samples once for every layer. Free, and it breaks the low frequency only
Dual2a second scale overlaid. The default, and the right answer for most layers
Hex3stochastic hex-tile sampling. No repeat survives it, at any distance

Dual scale

The same texture sampled again at an incommensurate scale and combined as an overlay, not a lerp. That distinction is the whole trick: a lerp of two scales halves the contrast and reads as blur, trading one artefact for another. Overlay keeps the detail and only disturbs the low frequency that made the repeat visible.

SecondScale0.37, not 0.5. Keep it awkward, or the two repeats line up and you have built a larger grid
Amounthow far the second tiling pushes. 1 is fine; drop it if the layer starts to look noisy

On by default on every landscape layer. Turn it off only for a layer that never covers enough screen to repeat.

Stochastic hex tiling

The lattice is hexagonal, not square. Each cell samples the texture with its own random offset and rotation, and every pixel blends the three cells it falls between - so the pattern that repeats is the hex lattice, which has no visual signature of its own, rather than the texture.

This is the modern form of what used to be called texture bombing or cell bombing. Bombing stamped decorated patches over a base and left visible patch edges and a stamp rate to tune. Hex tiling has no base, no stamps, and no seams - every pixel is a blend of three taps and the cost is fixed at three, whatever the content.

Use itDo not
The one or two layers covering the most screen: grass, dirt, sandOn every layer. Three taps each adds up fast, and a layer painted in patches never repeats anyway
Ground seen from a distance where the grid would otherwise be unmissableTextures with strong directional features - planks, bricks, tiles. Random rotation destroys them
Large open terrain with few layersMeshes. The surface master uses the distance tiling break instead
Blending three taps averages the texture's variance away, so the layer comes out washed out and sits flat next to everything else. VarianceRestore pushes the result back out from the mean, and MeanColor has to be that texture's actual average colour - the shader cannot know it. Set it wrong and the layer shifts hue as well as contrast.
MeanColorthe source texture's average. Sample it in your image editor at the smallest mip
VarianceRestore1 restores fully. Lower it if the result looks over-contrasted rather than merely un-washed
RotationAmount1 is full random rotation. Drop toward 0 for a texture with a direction it needs to keep, but then expect less of the repeat to be hidden
BlendContrast4. How sharply the three cells hand over. Too low and cells cross-fade into mush; too high and cell edges appear
Every tier takes explicit gradients. The layer samplers are Shared:Wrap, so an implicit sample inside a Custom node takes its derivatives from the wrong place once UVs are rotated or offset per cell, and mip selection fights the coordinates across a cell seam. If you see mip seams tracing hex cells, a tap has lost its gradients.

Distance tiling break

The mesh equivalent, and it is bound to distance on purpose: a repeat only becomes visible once enough tiles are on screen at once. Near, the surface keeps the detail it was authored with. Far, the grid dissolves.

TileBreakScale0.37, 0.41, 0.53 per layer by default. Irrational-ish, for the same reason as dual scale
TileBreakStart / TileBreakFalloff1500 / 4000 cm. Move Start in if the grid is visible sooner than that; it usually is on a large flat floor
TileBreakAmount0.7. Push to 1 for a surface that is nothing but tiling

Mesh-UV only. With triplanar on it does nothing, deliberately: that already samples three ways, and breaking it too would be nine taps to solve a repeat the projection has largely hidden. Three extra samples on any layer that uses it.

Macro variation

One low-frequency world-space noise, sampled once for the whole material and used by every layer. It is the only variation source that changes across a single mesh, which is what a long wall, a building facade or a whole hillside needs.

MacroNoiseTexturea large, soft greyscale noise tiled very low. The plugin ships a flat 4x4 placeholder, so this is the most common "the feature does nothing" cause. /Engine/EngineMaterials/T_Default_MacroVariation works to start
Variation_NoiseScale0.002 - roughly a 5 m feature. Halve it for a landscape read, double it for props
MacroTintA / MacroTintBthe two colours the noise crossfades between. This is where a palette gets applied at world scale
Variation_MacroTintAmount0.5. Hue movement
Variation_MacroValueAmount0.25. Value movement. Keep this below the tint amount unless you want the world to look cloudy

Height blending

The blend between two layers is resolved against their height rather than lerped, so gravel comes up through grass along its own stones and mortar fills before brick. Cavity is the height source, so no fourth texture is needed.

BlendHeightAmount0 is a plain cross-fade, 1 is a pure interlock. 0.45 realistic, 0.85 stylized
BlendContrasthow wide a band the interlock happens over. Low is a hard crunchy edge. Raising contrast is how you soften an edge - lowering the amount just returns you to a cross-fade
LayerN_HeightBiaspushes one layer up or down the blend, so it wins in more or fewer places without repainting
This is the single highest-value thing on the page, and it is free - the height is already sampled. A scene where every transition interlocks reads as built rather than painted.

Detail normals

A second, finer normal laid over the blended result and faded out on the same curve the distance clamp uses, because it is exactly the high-frequency detail that crawls at range.

Use itDo not
Anything the player gets within a couple of metres ofTerrain seen only from a distance
Large surfaces at low texel density, to buy back the close-up readSurfaces already at 1024 px/m, where it only adds noise
DetailNormala fine, high-frequency tiling normal. Defaults to a flat placeholder
DetailScale8 tiles per base tile. Raise until the detail stops being individually readable
DetailStrength0.5 realistic, 0.25 stylized

One extra sample for the whole material, not per layer.

Parallax

Shifts the UV along the view direction so a flat surface reads as having depth. Neither mode changes the silhouette - at a grazing angle the edge is still flat, which is the honest limit of the trick.

ModeCostUse it
Offset (LayerN_Parallax)a multiply and an addfreely. It sells brick, cobbles, plank floors and anything with shallow, regular depth
Occlusion (LayerN_ParallaxOcclusion)a sampler slotone hero surface in the level, if that. A raymarch cannot be expressed as graph taps, so its Custom node brings its own sampler - the only feature in either master that does
ParallaxAmount0.04. Past about 0.1 the swimming at grazing angles costs more than the depth buys
ParallaxSteps16, occlusion only. Fewer steps stair-step on steep height changes

Leave Parallax off on the recipe if nothing needs it and it is not in the master at all.

Per-instance variation

Three tools, and picking the wrong one is how projects end up with forty material instances that differ by a tint.

VariesUse it when
bColorVariationper object, hashed from world positionyou want every copy to differ and do not care how. Crates, barrels, roof tiles. No per-actor setup at all
bMacroVariationacross the world, including across one mesha single large object or a whole area needs to stop being one tone
bPrimitiveDataper actor, set by youa specific object needs a specific tint, roughness or wetness, from Blueprint or the details panel

The hash gives every copy a different tint. Primitive data gives a copy the tint somebody chose. Neither adds a shader permutation, which is the point: a new material instance does.

Custom primitive data indices are a fixed contract, asserted by Verify Contract: tint 0-3, roughness 4, wetness 5. Set Num Custom Data Floats to 6 on the component.

RVT blending

The landscape writes its finished blend into a runtime virtual texture. Two things fall out of that, and they are worth separating.

The terrain samples its own cacheper-pixel cost stops scaling with layer count, so twelve layers cost what one does. This is what makes a large layer set affordable
Meshes sample it tooa rock reads the ground it is standing in and fades into it, so it meets the terrain instead of cutting a silhouette. Repaint the ground and everything standing in it follows

Turn on Build Project Outputs, run Mat → Wire Landscape Runtime Virtual Textures, then add MF_<AssetName>RVTBlend to the mesh's own material.

bUseRVT on with nothing writing the texture samples zero: base colour 0 and roughness 0, so the terrain is black and mirror-smooth whatever the layers say. If virtual texturing is off in the project, turn bUseRVT off too.

The distance clamp

Not optional and not a style choice. With FXAA and no temporal filter, sub-pixel normal and specular detail crawls in motion, and the material is the only place it can be removed. Always on, no switch.

FadeStart / FadeLength2000 / 6000 cm on the surface master
DistanceNormalFlatten0.55 realistic, 0.75 stylized. How flat the normal goes at range
DistanceRoughnessFloor0.35 realistic, 0.5 stylized. The lowest roughness allowed at range, which is what kills the sparkle

On TAA instead of FXAA, turn both down - the temporal filter is doing this job and the clamp is only costing you detail.

Judge this in motion, at ES3.1, with FXAA on. A screenshot cannot show you the problem it solves.

Texture arrays

Past about four landscape layers, every layer becomes one slice of three arrays instead of three textures of its own: eight layers is three texture objects rather than twenty-four.

  1. Tick Texture Array Layers on the recipe and set Layer Texture Root.
  2. Mat → Pack Layers for <Recipe>.
  3. Generate.

Every layer's textures then have to be the same resolution and format, which is the real constraint. Detail on Landscape.

What to turn on first

If you do exactly four things to a scene that already has art in it, do these.

  1. BlendHeightAmount above 0 on every blend.
  2. Dual scale on the terrain, tiling break on the meshes.
  3. A real noise in MacroNoiseTexture.
  4. RVT blend on anything sitting in the ground.
